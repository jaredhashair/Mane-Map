How Much Does a Hair System Really Cost in 2026?

Pricing for hair systems varies a lot and isn't always easy to pin down. Here's what they actually cost, broken down by how hands-on you want to be, plus the costs most guys don't find out about until later.

Quick answer

Most guys land somewhere between $100 and $362 a month depending on maintenance style, with year-one totals ranging from $1,500 to $6,700. The biggest cost driver isn't the system itself. It's whether you maintain it yourself or a salon does it every visit.

Cost by maintenance style

This is the single biggest factor in what you'll actually spend. Every hair system needs periodic removal, cleaning, and reattachment. The question is who does that work.

StyleMonthly rangeWhat's included
Full DIY$100–$175You remove, clean, and reattach at home. System + adhesive/products + monthly haircut. No salon visits.
Mixed (partial DIY)$155–$250Salon installs your system, you maintain it at home between visits, typically every 10–14 days.
Full salon$250–$362The salon handles every removal, clean, and reattachment. Simplest route, highest ongoing cost.

Year-one total cost

Year one is always the most expensive. You're paying for the initial system, the learning curve, and often more frequent adjustments while you and your specialist dial in the right fit. Costs typically drop after that.

Cost by base type

The base material you choose also affects cost, mainly through how often it needs replacing.

Thin skin poly

The most natural-looking hairline, best for exposed styles like slicked-back or brushed-up looks. Replaces every 2–3 months, which makes it the most frequent (and over time, more expensive) replacement schedule of the three.

Thicker poly (0.06–0.08mm)

Better suited to obscured or partially exposed hairlines: forward, tousled, messier styles. Lasts 4–5 months, meaningfully stretching your replacement cost over the year.

Lace or hybrid

The most breathable option, best for active lifestyles and heavy sweating. Also lasts 4–5 months, but requires more careful at-home maintenance than poly if you're doing any of the upkeep yourself.

The hidden costs nobody mentions upfront

  • Adhesive and removers: roughly $25/month if you're doing any maintenance yourself, regardless of which route you choose.
  • Monthly haircuts: around $25/month, though this is largely offsetting what you were likely already spending before.
  • The first few adjustment visits: it's common to need a follow-up appointment or two early on while the fit gets dialed in, which isn't always included in the sticker price you're quoted.
  • Travel: if there's no vetted specialist near you, factor in the cost of traveling to one who's actually good, rather than defaulting to whoever's closest.

Common cost questions

Why is year one so much more expensive?
You're covering the initial system, learning the maintenance routine, and often a couple of early adjustment visits while the fit gets dialed in. Most wearers settle into a lower, steadier monthly cost after that.
Is full DIY actually worth the savings?
It's the cheapest route by a wide margin, but it comes with a real learning curve and a time commitment. Expect around 1.5 hours per maintenance session once you're comfortable with the process.
Does the base type change the total cost that much?
Yes. A thin skin poly base replaced every 2–3 months costs meaningfully more over a year than a thicker poly or lace base lasting 4–5 months, even if the per-system price is similar.
